emet typography, T1 to T4

The four run-1 pairings applied to the real wordmark emet, lowercase, from the golem legend. Each card sets the word at four sizes, in five weights and five tracking values, then shows README text, the /emet:run command in the mono face and a terminal sample. All fonts are OFL, loaded from local fonts/ files. These are options, not a decision.

Recommendation: T3 Geist + Geist Mono, wordmark at weight 600

The mark has to survive 16 px and sit in a terminal first. Geist keeps the e aperture open and the m wide at small sizes, which is the main risk in a word made of two e's, an m and a t. One family across UI and mono means the README and the terminal look like the same tool, which fits a sober, precise voice.

Its weakness is anonymity and a resemblance to Vercel. The identity should come from the palette and the mascot, and the humour from the copy. If the maintainer wants the type to carry more character, T2 is the runner-up, with tracking loosened below 24 px.

No pairing was swapped: none of the faces already in fonts/ was clearly better for this word, and adding one needs a woff2 plus a licence line. A geometric face with a single-storey, open e (for example a Sora or Outfit style) is worth testing in a later run.

Letterform and flaw notes come from general knowledge of these faces and were not checked against a rendered screenshot (no browser was available when this sheet was built). Verify them on the 16 px tiles on your own display before deciding.

Letterforms
  • e: round bowl, horizontal bar, open aperture. The two e's are identical, so the double e reads as a deliberate rhythm.
  • m: medium width, two even arches. Reads as a block, neither narrow nor spread.
  • t: short crossbar that sits at x-height, with a small curved foot. The foot is the one detail that survives at 16 px.
Flaws

Rounded terminals look soft for a tool whose voice is dry. At 16 px and weight 800 the e aperture closes, so e can read as c or o. Fix: use weight 700 and +0.01em tracking below 20 px.

Letterforms
  • e: flat-cut terminals and a horizontal bar. The cut gives the double e an engineered, stamped look.
  • m: slightly narrower than T1, with squared shoulders. It is the most compact of the four.
  • t: crossbar extends further left than right and the stem is cut flat, which makes the t the most distinctive letter in the word.
Flaws

The most character, so the most likely to date. At -0.045em the m and e touch below 24 px. Space Grotesk is also on the design guide's list of safe, generic picks. Fix: tracking no tighter than -0.02em under 24 px.

Letterforms
  • e: open, slightly squarish bowl. The aperture stays open at small sizes, the best of the four against e/c confusion.
  • m: wide, with clear internal counters. It carries the mark at 16 px.
  • t: tall stem, crossbar at x-height, a clean hook. Close to the Vercel look, which is the risk.
Flaws

Looks like a Vercel product at a glance, so it adds no identity of its own. Neutral to the point of anonymous; the dry humour has to come from the copy, not the type.

Letterforms
  • e: oval bowl with an angled terminal. The double e is the most calligraphic of the four.
  • m: narrow and tall. Words made of m and e stay compact, which suits a four-letter mark.
  • t: crossbar is thin relative to the stem at 800, so the t can look like a plain l with a nick at 16 px.
Flaws

At 16 px the heavy weight fills the e counter and the thin t crossbar nearly vanishes, so it reads as emel. Needs weight 700 or lower and positive tracking at small sizes.
