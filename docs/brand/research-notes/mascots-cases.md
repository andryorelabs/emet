# Mascot research: raw notes

Raw notes behind [`mascot-research.md`](../mascot-research.md). Every source was
accessed on 2026-11-04 unless a line says otherwise. Quotes are copied from the
page as fetched; anything not confirmed on a primary source is marked.

## Octocat (GitHub)

- Source: [Octodex FAQ](https://octodex.github.com/faq/). Using an octocat "to
  refer to GitHub" or as a personal avatar is fine; it is "not for your company
  or a product you're building", and "using an Octocat as your logo or icon is
  not allowed". "The OCTOCAT design is the exclusive property of GitHub, Inc."
  and is a registered trademark.
- Source: [GitHub brand, logo](https://brand.github.com/foundations/logo). "We
  call our logo pictogram the Invertocat — it represents our mascot, the
  Octocat." The mark appears only "in white, black, or in few cases grey or
  green". "Don't use our illustration, mascots or Mona as a substitute for the
  logo." No minimum size is stated on the page.
- Source: [Cameron McEfee, The Octocat](https://cameronmcefee.com/work/the-octocat/)
  (the illustrator's own portfolio). The founders bought a stock illustration
  called "Octopuss" for error pages; McEfee gave it a story from 2011. The
  Octodex started as a blog of his variants. Fixed traits: five appendages
  (matching the silhouette and negative space of the mark), the character never
  speaks, problem-solving props are goldenrod, she is an optimistic
  "any-person". Fan art was allowed when linked to the fan's GitHub profile or
  project.
- Not confirmed on a primary source: the original stock artist's name and the
  purchase year. Secondary sources disagree on 2007 versus 2008; left out of the
  study.

## Go gopher

- Source: [The Go Gopher, go.dev blog](https://go.dev/blog/gopher) (2014). Drawn
  by Renee French, first for a WFMU radio fundraiser T-shirt; adapted into the
  mascot for the 2009 open-source launch. It has no name beyond "the Go gopher".
  Images are "Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licensed"; adaptations must
  credit Renee French. The logo and the mascot are separate artefacts.
- Source: [Go brand](https://go.dev/brand). "The Go Gopher mascot was created by
  Renee French and is licensed under the Creative Commons 4.0 Attribution
  License." The Go Logo is a Google trademark and "may not be used with events,
  community groups, or other gatherings ... without permission".
- Adoption, from the GitHub API on 2026-11-04:
  [egonelbre/gophers](https://github.com/egonelbre/gophers), "Free gophers",
  CC0-1.0, 3,846 stars;
  [ashleymcnamara/gophers](https://github.com/ashleymcnamara/gophers), "Gopher
  Artwork by Ashley McNamara", license reported as "Other", 3,079 stars.

## Ferris (Rust, unofficial)

- Source: [rustacean.net](https://rustacean.net/). Ferris is the "unofficial
  mascot for Rust". Karen Rustad Tölva "has waived all copyright and related or
  neighboring rights to Ferris the Rustacean" (CC0 1.0). Variants: original,
  flat, cuddly, happy, gesturing, plus a side character (Corro the Unsafe
  Rusturchin). The name plays on "ferrous" and "rustacean"; the page does not
  explain the crab choice beyond the pun.

## Tux (Linux)

- Source: [The Linux Foundation, the Linux mark](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/the-linux-mark).
  "Tux the Penguin is an image created by Larry Ewing, and is not owned by The
  Linux Foundation." The page refers usage questions to Larry Ewing's page.
- Larry Ewing's page (`isc.tamu.edu/~lewing/linux/`) returned HTTP 404 on
  2026-11-04, and the archive copy could not be fetched from this machine. The
  permission text ("Permission to use and/or modify this image is granted
  provided you acknowledge me ... and The GIMP if someone asks") and the 1996
  date and GIMP 0.54 tool come from the secondary
  [Wikipedia article](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tux_(mascot)>); not checked
  against the original.

## Moby Dock (Docker)

- Source: [Call me Moby Dock, Docker blog](https://www.docker.com/blog/call-me-moby-dock/)
  (2013-10-25). The community named the whale through a survey with 411 votes.
- Source: [Docker trademark guidelines](https://www.docker.com/legal/trademark-guidelines/).
  The "Moby Dock" whale logo is a Docker mark. "Do not create imitations,
  facsimiles or reproductions of these design elements on your own"; the logo
  "may not be modified, recombined or displayed in modified scenes"; mark use
  "as an avatar" on social media "is not permitted"; the Apache 2.0 code license
  grants no right to the marks.
- Source: [Docker media resources](https://www.docker.com/company/newsroom/media-resources/).
  Logos "should be at least 24px"; "Don't decolorize"; only Docker brand blues
  or white.

## Bun

- Source: [Bun press kit](https://bun.com/press-kit). Logo, wordmark and an
  icon "for small sizes"; colours Cream `#FBF0DF`, Pink `#F472B6`, Ink
  `#14151A`, Blush `#FEBBD0`. Usage: "Please don't stretch, recolour or put a
  hat on the bun without asking."
- Source: [bun.com/logo.svg](https://bun.com/logo.svg), inspected on
  2026-11-04. Its layer ids include `Bun`, `Face`, `Eyes`, `Iris`, `Mouth`,
  `Tongue`, `Left_Bluch` and `Right_Blush`: the logo itself is the character,
  with a face drawn on the product-name object.

## Deno

- Source: [Deno artwork gallery](https://deno.com/artwork). About 29
  community pieces, each credited to its artist with a licence label: mostly
  MIT, two CC-BY-SA-4.0, three "Restricted use". The logo is a small dinosaur.
- Source: [denoland/deno_website2 README](https://github.com/denoland/deno_website2).
  "These Deno images are distributed under the MIT license (public domain and
  free for use)."

## Claude Code terminal mascot ("Clawd")

- Source: [anthropics/claude-code issue 24926](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/24926)
  (closed 2026-03-12). A small block-character mascot is printed when Claude
  Code launches; the issue quotes its three-line form.
- The name "Clawd", its crab reading and any design rationale: no Anthropic
  primary source found on 2026-11-04 (searched anthropic.com, claude.com and the
  anthropics GitHub org). Treated as community usage only.
- Source: [Anthropic trademark guidelines](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/trademark-guidelines).
  "Our trademarks include our names, logos, brand elements, and anything we use
  to identify our goods and services." No use "in a manner that implies
  Anthropic's sponsorship or endorsement".
- Source: [OpenClaw lore](https://docs.openclaw.ai/start/lore). The project's
  lobster mascot was called Clawd in "Clawdbot" (2025-11-25 to 2026-01-27).
  Anthropic sent a "polite email asking for a name change" citing trademark
  concerns; the project became Moltbot, then OpenClaw on 2026-01-30.

## Slonik (PostgreSQL)

- Source: [PostgreSQL wiki, Logo](https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/Logo). The
  elephant is "Slonik"; the idea is traced to a pgsql-hackers thread ("elephants
  can remember"). Single-colour black, blue and white versions and a favicon
  are offered.
- Source: [PostgreSQL trademark policy](https://www.postgresql.org/about/policies/trademarks/).
  Slonik is a registered trademark of the PostgreSQL Community Association of
  Canada; it "must appear in one of the forms below" (colour and black-and-white)
  and "should not be modified in any way without prior approval".

## Huggy (Hugging Face)

- Source: [Hugging Face brand](https://huggingface.co/brand). Logo files
  include a monochrome logo and a "Pirate" logo; a Huggy collection (Coding,
  Vibing, Chef) sits in a separate asset universe. Colours `#FFD21E`, `#FF9D00`,
  `#6B7280`. No usage rules are stated on the page.
